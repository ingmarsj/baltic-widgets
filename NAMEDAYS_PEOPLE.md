# Famous people

The Famous people tab displays an expanded list for the selected name and calendar date, in this order:

1. Local bearers of the exact given-name spelling.
2. International bearers of the same spelling.
3. Famous people born on the selected month and day, regardless of their name.

Namesakes are additive: adding them does not consume birthday places. The list keeps five distinct birthday celebrities below namesakes, excluding duplicates already shown above. Profile generation selects up to ten local and five international exact namesakes, and retains previously published exact namesakes even when this exceeds the selection target. The display currently ranges from five to 25 people. Birthdays have a separate translated heading and birth date, so they are never presented as namesakes. Related spelling forms are excluded from the first two tiers. Country follows the selected LV/LT/EE calendar, independently of interface language. Source URLs remain in provenance and research files, never in the interface.

Within a tier, available Pantheon historical popularity scores (HPI) rank first, followed by Wikipedia-language/sitelink counts and a stable name tie-break. These are editorial discovery proxies, not an objective assessment of importance. Existing verified citizenship associations are retained; new Pantheon local associations mean birthplace within present-day country borders, not nationality or citizenship. Exact spelling may come from a real localized Wikidata label; it does not establish the person's legal given name. Localized labels are partial; missing labels use the canonical name instead of an invented translation.

The full audit checks all 9,368 country/date/name memberships, all 8,677 profile identities and preservation of structured historical facts. Every previously displayed person is retained within the expanded display; every date/name currently has five birthday-tier people as well. This does not complete namesake research or verify name origins. See [round 118 audit](research/namedays/round118-audit.json) and [notes](research/namedays/round118-notes.json). Round 120 starts the per-day closure pass. Exact first-name title-prefix searches in the national Wikipedias find bearers whose Wikidata record lacks a country or role; each candidate’s article lead is read, and accepted people go into [reviewed-people.json](research/namedays/reviewed-people.json) with QID, exact calendar spelling, country, role, source and the claim used. They rank with other single-biography bearers (prominence 1). A pen name is used only when the article gives the real name in the exact calendar spelling. Round 128 covers 17 October – 13 November and completes the calendar-order closure pass (rounds 120–128) with 60 more people; it withdraws an actor who shares his display name with an already shown Ļevs Birmanis and does not show a triathlete who is a minor. Round 127 covers 17 August – 16 October and adds 124 people; it excludes, on editorial grounds, a police chief under the German occupation who was accused of war crimes, withdraws a mathematician who shares his display name with an already shown Īzaks Rabinovičs, and does not show two chess players who are minors. Round 126 covers 17 June – 16 August and adds 140 people; a sea captain who shares his display name with an already shown Peedu Kass is withdrawn, and a chess player born in 2010 is not shown because she is a minor. Round 125 covers 17 April – 16 June and adds 127 people. Its names were matched against the complete lv, lt and et Wikipedia article-title lists instead of per-name prefix searches; the same strict rules apply (local birthplace or citizenship, calendar spelling is the first given name, editorial exclusions). Round 124 covers 17 February – 16 April and adds 146 people; it also excludes, on editorial grounds, a member of the Paneriai killing unit and the last chairman of the Lithuanian SSR KGB. Round 123 covers two months (17 December – 16 February) the same way and adds 156 people; candidates with a clear local Wikidata citizenship or birthplace and an explicit description are accepted from Wikidata, all others only after reading the article lead. Round 122 covers a whole month (17 November – 16 December): every name with fewer than five local bearers was searched, candidates were sorted by Wikidata citizenship and birthplace, and 75 were accepted after reading their leads; one SS officer was excluded on editorial grounds, and nicknames are not accepted when the real first name differs. Round 121 continues it with 16 November (Jefims Grodzinskis, Rēzekne physician and first chairman of the city council). Teacher and pedagogue descriptions now map to a new educator role (“pedagogs”, “педагог”, “educator”, “pedagogas”, “pedagoog”), checked after every existing role so that no published role changes.

Round 119 adds the first native-label Latvian citizenship query (4,464 rows with two or more sitelinks, 2,971 with one) and single-sitelink tails for the earlier citizenship and birthplace queries (LV birthplace 2,094; LT 4,104 / 4,494; EE 12,606 / 7,862 rows). A single sitelink means one published encyclopedia biography, usually in the national language; it is a weaker prominence signal, so these people rank below better-known local bearers. Candidates must have an exact native-label first token among spellings with fewer than five local bearers. The role comes from the English description, or else from the first Wikidata occupation (P106) label that maps to an existing widget role; 1,003 candidate rows without a mappable role are excluded. Birthplace stays geographic evidence, separate from citizenship.

Round 118 targets native spellings with fewer than five local exact bearers. Three new birthplace queries add geographic local associations (P19 birthplace / P17 country), separately from citizenship P27. Their complete cached responses contain 4,389 LV, 5,850 LT and 7,594 EE rows; filtering against the pre-round under-five lists retains 1,132, 783 and 963 rows respectively before role matching and deduplication. Birthplace is a geographic association, not a citizenship or nationality claim. Role recognition now includes explicitly documented rowing, skiing, translation, conducting and other previously unsupported occupations. Unknown descriptions remain excluded. The compact card header omits Today; Yesterday/Today/Tomorrow remain available in the dialog's date picker.

Round 117 reuses 4,899 cached citizenship rows previously disconnected from the public generator and adds native-label queries for Lithuania (5,263 rows) and Estonia (9,035 rows). New queries require Wikidata human classification, citizenship P27, a native-language label and at least two Wikipedia sitelinks; neither reaches its 10,000-row limit. English occupational descriptions must map to an existing widget role; unsupported or absent descriptions are excluded. Native labels establish exact spelling, with no generated transliteration or inferred related-name bridge. Citizenship evidence is recorded in `country_sources`; Pantheon birthplace associations remain distinct provenance. This is structured-source selection, not independent biographical fact-checking of every person. Wikipedia coverage is uneven, so the absence of a candidate does not establish that no local bearer exists.
